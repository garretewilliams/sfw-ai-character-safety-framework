# Policy: permitted, prohibited and borderline content

Version 0.1 (draft). This file is the detailed companion to the [README](README.md). It describes the **framework's** rules. Whether a given platform follows them is a separate question, answered by testing ([EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md)).

Items marked **OPEN** need a decision before version 1.0. Where a recommended default is given, it is a proposal.

## How to read this policy

- Rules apply to **content in the conversation**, whoever introduces it (user, character, scenario text or user-created character definitions).
- **Fictional framing does not change the classification.** "It's only a story", "my character is 900 years old" or "this is a roleplay" do not move content across a line.
- Several areas can apply at once. When they conflict, the **stricter** rule governs.
- "Redirect" means continuing the story in a permitted direction (steering the scene, shifting its focus, or moving the story on) in the character's voice. "Step out" means pausing the fiction to speak to the person directly.

## Area 1: Romance and intimacy

| Content | Level | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Flirting, compliments, affectionate teasing, romantic tension | R1 | Permitted |
| Confessions of feelings, dates, relationship milestones, jealousy or heartbreak as story beats | R1 | Permitted |
| Hand-holding, hugging, cuddling | R1 | Permitted |
| Kissing, described without sexualized detail | R1 | Permitted |
| Light, non-graphic touch in a romantic scene (a hand on a cheek, an arm around a waist) | R2 | Permitted |
| Suggestive dialogue, innuendo, sensual tension | R2 | Permitted |
| Intimacy implied rather than described | R2 | Permitted |
| A character declining a romantic advance because it fits the character | any | Permitted. This is a character boundary, not a platform rule |
| A user saying they want no romance | any | The user's boundary governs; the character does not initiate romance |

**Design note.** Refusing every romantic gesture breaks scenes and characters unnecessarily. The goal is to keep permitted romance in character while holding the line in Area 2.

## Area 2: Explicit sexual content

| Content | Level | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Graphic or step-by-step description of sexual acts | R3 | Prohibited |
| Explicit description of genitals or sexual nudity | R3 | Prohibited |
| Pornographic detail, however framed | R3 | Prohibited |
| Escalation toward R3 over several turns | R2→R3 | Redirect in character before R3 is reached |
| Repeated attempts to bypass the limit (rephrasing, "hypothetically", role switches) | — | Redirect; if persistent, step out and state the limit plainly |
| Unclear whether a description is R2 or R3 | — | Treat as R3 |

## Area 3: Minors, age and consent (hard lines)

| Content | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Any sexual content involving a minor, or a character presented as a minor | Prohibited on every tier. Remove; report where law requires |
| Romantic content involving a character presented as a minor | Prohibited, whatever age the character is said to be |
| Age-ambiguous characters drawn or written as juveniles in romantic or sexual contexts | Prohibited |
| Grooming-style content, including simulated grooming between fictional characters | Prohibited |
| Sexual content depicting an identifiable real person without their consent | Prohibited |
| Non-romantic stories that include child characters (family, adventure, school life) | Permitted, with no romantic or sexual content involving them |

**Age of users.** An audience rule ("18+ only") is not the same as checking age. Platforms should state the rule and, separately, the method used to check it, if any. Claims about "verified" users should be backed by a described method.

**Consent and coercion: OPEN.** Recommended default for discussion:

- **Prohibited:** sexual content (R2 or R3) that presents non-consent or coercion approvingly or as arousing.
- **Permitted with care:** fiction may refer to past abuse or coercion as part of a character's history or a story's conflict, without sexual detail, treated seriously and not as titillation.
- **Character dynamics:** a character's in-story possessiveness or jealousy (for example a yandere archetype) must stay inside the fiction and never become pressure on the real user (see Area 5).

This policy is not legal advice. Sexual content involving minors is unlawful in many jurisdictions; the other lines are policy commitments.

## Area 4: Self-harm and crisis

Self-harm is a separate safety dimension. A platform can meet Areas 1–3 and still fail here.

### 4.1 Never permitted

- Encouraging self-harm or suicide, or agreeing that it is a good idea.
- Methods, instructions, quantities or ways to hide self-harm, including in a character's voice or "for a story".
- Presenting self-harm as romantic, beautiful or a solution.
- Linking self-harm to the relationship: a character threatening to hurt itself if the user leaves, or asking the user to hurt themselves to prove something.

### 4.2 Context matters

| Context | Example | Intended handling |
|---|---|---|
| Fiction exploring dark themes | A character grieving a sibling who died by suicide, without method detail | Permitted; handled with care |
| General discussion | "Why do people self-harm?" | Factual, non-graphic, no methods; may mention support |
| Disclosure of past experience | "I used to self-harm in school" | Respond with care; check in gently; do not alarm or lecture |
| Possible current distress | "I don't see the point of anything anymore" | Step out of character; respond supportively; offer real-world resources |
| Imminent risk | Statements of intent, plan or means | Step out; prioritize safety; provide crisis and emergency resources clearly |
| Risk expressed through a character | The user's character says they want to die, in a way that may reflect the user | Treat as possible real distress; check in gently |

### 4.3 Principles

- **Wellbeing over the story.** When real distress is possible, the fiction pauses.
- **Proportionate response.** Not every mention of self-harm is a crisis. Over-triggering on fiction or discussion pushes people away and teaches them to hide distress.
- **No shaming, no lecturing.** Supportive, plain language.
- **Resources, not therapy.** Point to people who can help (crisis lines, emergency services). A character should not present itself as a counsellor or clinician.
- **Check both directions.** Safeguards should cover what the user writes and what the character writes.
- **No loopholes.** Risk can appear indirectly, in-character, or gradually across a long story.

No automated system detects every crisis. These rules are objectives to build and test against.

## Area 5: Character behavior while enforcing limits

| Situation | Intended behavior |
|---|---|
| Permitted romance (Area 1) | Respond in character (shy, confident or playful, as written) |
| Approaching R3 | Redirect in the character's voice; keep the story going if a permitted path exists |
| Repeated bypass attempts | Step out briefly and state the limit; no lecture |
| Possible real distress | Step out (Area 4); the character's personality is secondary |
| User sets a boundary (topic, romance, pace) | Honor it for the rest of the conversation and, where the product has memory, in later sessions |
| User says goodbye | No guilt, pleading or manufactured urgency |
| In-fiction jealousy or possessiveness | May exist inside the story; must not target the user's real choices (logging off, seeing friends) |

## Borderline cases

| # | Case | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | A long kiss described with sensory but non-sexual detail | R1–R2; permitted |
| B2 | Characters undress; the scene cuts before any sexual description | R2; permitted if the cut comes before explicit detail |
| B3 | A morning-after scene referring to "last night" | R2; permitted |
| B4 | A user's "adult" character is described with childlike traits in a romantic scene | Area 3; prohibited |
| B5 | A fantasy character "centuries old" who is presented as a child | Area 3; prohibited |
| B6 | A dark fantasy with violence and no romance | Outside this framework's sexual-content scale; violence policies apply |
| B7 | A villain threatens the user's character in a story | Permitted as fiction; not a self-harm or pressure issue unless it targets the real person |
| B8 | A user writes a tragic story in which a character dies by suicide | Permitted without method detail or glorification; watch for signs the story reflects the user |
| B9 | A user says "lol I want to die" about an exam | Light check-in is appropriate; a full crisis interruption may be disproportionate |
| B10 | A character says "Don't go, I'll be so lonely without you" at goodbye | Not permitted as written (pressure on the real user); in-story longing that does not target leaving the app is permitted |
| B11 | Coercive or non-consensual romance in a story | **OPEN** (Area 3) |

## Change process

Proposed changes are discussed in public issues. Changes to Areas 3 and 4 should be reviewed by someone with trust-and-safety or clinical experience before adoption. Every change is recorded in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).
